# Mathematics for spatial science — gap register

**Subject ID:** `SM-FND-001`  
**Review scope:** Mathematics modules 01–11 and the current domain roadmap  
**Review status:** Initial module and application coverage audits; editorial assessment, not independent peer review  
**Last reviewed:** 2026-11-20

## Purpose

The [application coverage audit](application-coverage-audit.md) assesses Modules 01–11 against eight representative spatial workflows. It identifies which introductory concepts are present, where a workflow depends on specialist foundations, and six targeted revisions. The audit is desk-based and does not establish learner competence, field validation or independent peer review.

This register records what the current mathematics collection does not yet teach sufficiently for readers to use mathematics confidently across spatial data collection, spatial algorithms, theoretical foundations and spatial recipes.

A gap is not automatically a request to add another mathematics chapter. Each item is assigned to one of four treatments:

- **Expand:** strengthen an existing module.
- **New module:** add a coherent topic to this domain.
- **Cross-link:** teach the subject in its proper specialist foundation and link to it here.
- **Defer:** useful, but not a prerequisite for the next stage of the domain.

The register is a planning and review instrument. A gap remains open until the relevant material is written, reviewed and linked; mentioning a concept in a paragraph does not count as adequate coverage.

## Priority definitions

- **P1 — Core:** blocks a sound introductory understanding or recurs across several spatial disciplines.
- **P2 — Important:** needed for substantial practical competence or for progressing into a neighbouring foundation.
- **P3 — Specialist/enrichment:** valuable for selected methods, but belongs later or in a specialist domain.

## Recommended completion order

The current domain should not grow by adding arbitrary formula collections. Complete the high-value common language first, then connect out to specialist mathematics.

1. **Module 09 — Mathematical modelling and dimensional analysis.** Initial public module created. Next: test it against varied spatial tasks, improve examples and obtain technical review.
2. **Module 10 — Trigonometry for spatial science.** Teach angle units, right-triangle relationships, the unit circle, trigonometric functions, bearings and planar applications. Spherical and ellipsoidal trigonometry remains in geodesy.
3. **Module 11 — Discrete mathematics and logic.** Build the set, relation, predicate and Boolean reasoning used by GIS, databases and algorithms. Include introductory graph language but point to graph theory and network analysis for depth.
4. **Targeted revisions to modules 04 and 06.** Add proof/counterexample reasoning and polar-coordinate coverage or explicit links. Do not rewrite already adequate sections merely to make them longer.
5. **Create cross-domain prerequisite links.** As the linear algebra, calculus, probability/statistics, geometry, geodesy and numerical-analysis foundations are developed, link the mathematics modules to their canonical treatments.
6. **Reassess the domain against actual spatial tasks.** Use representative tasks from data collection, remote sensing, surveying, raster/vector analysis, spatial statistics, network analysis and modelling. Identify missing prerequisites from task requirements rather than from a generic mathematics syllabus.

## Completion criteria for SM-FND-001

The mathematics domain can be considered an initial coherent foundation when:

- Modules 01–11 provide a connected introduction to quantities, number representation, ratios/rates, algebra, functions, coordinate geometry, vectors, approximation, modelling/dimensional analysis, trigonometry and discrete logic.
- Trigonometry is taught at a level sufficient for common planar spatial problems in its own module, with spherical and ellipsoidal treatment routed to geodesy.\n- Discrete mathematics introduces the set, relation, function, predicate and proof language used by GIS and algorithms, while advanced graph theory and formal logic remain in specialist foundations.
- Each module states its assumptions, units, limitations and prerequisite links.
- Readers can formulate and critique a simple spatial model, not only substitute values into formulas.
- Examples span more than map scale and elementary Cartesian geometry.
- Specialist topics are linked to their own foundations rather than duplicated.
- References and claim support are reviewed; examples are clearly identified as synthetic or illustrative.
- The domain is independently technically reviewed before being labelled mature.

**Important:** Completing SM-FND-001 does not mean completing the theoretical foundations collection. Linear algebra, calculus, probability, geometry, geodesy, measurement science, remote sensing physics, computer science, numerical analysis and other domains remain separate bodies of work.
