> A concise, evidence-led synthesis of a development, debate, method or research question. This is not a link dump or a claim to have completed a systematic review.

## The signal

In a few sentences, state what happened, what is being examined, or what question deserves attention. Include dates and scope where relevant.

## Why it matters

Explain the implications for spatial data, computation, measurement, analysis, infrastructure, policy or practice. Identify who or what may be affected without assuming a single audience.

Prioritise primary research, official documentation, standards, datasets and direct institutional sources where appropriate. Use secondary commentary to add context, not to substitute for underlying evidence.

## What is uncertain or contested?

Record disagreements, missing evidence, methodological weaknesses, unresolved questions and what would change the assessment.

## Connections

Link to relevant concepts, technologies, datasets, methods, applications and existing Spatial Methods explorations. Explain the relationship.

## Useful next move

Recommend one concrete next action: read a source, inspect a dataset, reproduce a result, compare methods, run an experiment, follow a policy development or open a new investigation. Make clear what the action will help establish.

## Publication checklist

- [ ] The central signal is accurately represented.
- [ ] Important claims have traceable sources.
- [ ] Publication dates and event dates are not confused.
- [ ] Evidence is separated from analysis and opinion.
- [ ] Counter-evidence and limitations are included.
- [ ] No unsupported prediction is presented as fact.
- [ ] Links, quotations and licences are handled appropriately.
- [ ] The dispatch adds interpretation or utility beyond a list of links.
