## 1. Frame the investigation

### Starting question
State the question in a way that can be explored or tested.

### Scope
- What is included?
- What is deliberately excluded?
- What assumptions are being made?
- What would count as a useful result?

### Intended outcome
Choose the output that best serves this investigation: a working example, comparison, map, notebook, model, reproducible workflow, explanation, teaching demonstration, instrument test, prototype or documented finding.

### Evidence mode
State what kind of evidence the activity produces:
- theoretical derivation;
- synthetic or simulated result;
- computation using recorded or public data;
- software behaviour or benchmark;
- measurement from a physical instrument;
- prototype test;
- independently validated operational result.

Do not describe one evidence mode as another. Simulation is not physical validation; a prototype demonstration is not proof of production readiness.

## 5. Method

Describe the workflow in ordered, reproducible steps.

1. **Prepare:** inputs, environment, dependencies, equipment and configuration.
2. **Calibrate / verify:** instrument checks, reference measurements, software tests or baseline validation as applicable.
3. **Inspect:** quality checks and exploratory examination.
4. **Execute:** method, parameters and decisions.
5. **Validate:** independent checks, expected behaviour and edge cases.
6. **Interpret:** what the output does and does not establish.

Include commands, code, equations, diagrams, wiring diagrams, procedures or screenshots where they materially improve reproducibility. Pin important software, hardware, firmware and data versions.

## 8. Critical examination

- What worked, and under what conditions?
- Where is the method sensitive to data, scale, parameters, calibration or assumptions?
- What are the error sources and uncertainty?
- What alternative methods would be reasonable?
- What would invalidate or materially change the conclusion?
- What does this experiment not demonstrate?

## 9. Adaptation and application

Describe one or more ways the method could be adapted to another place, dataset, discipline or operational problem. Identify what would need to change, including tools, access, calibration, validation and safety requirements.
