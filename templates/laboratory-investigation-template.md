# Laboratory Investigation

> A reusable structure for turning a question into a bounded, reproducible practical investigation. Adapt the sections to the subject; omit what does not apply.

## 1. Frame the investigation

### Starting question
State the question in a way that can be explored or tested.

### Scope
- What is included?
- What is deliberately excluded?
- What assumptions are being made?
- What would count as a useful result?

### Intended outcome
Choose the output that best serves this investigation: a working example, comparison, map, notebook, model, reproducible workflow, explanation, teaching demonstration, or documented finding.

## 2. Establish the foundations

List the concepts, methods, data, tools and prerequisites that matter. Explain why each is needed; do not turn this into a generic prerequisite syllabus.

Record dates, geographic and temporal coverage, coordinate reference systems, units, resolution, known limitations and any transformations applied.

## 4. Method

Describe the workflow in ordered, reproducible steps.

1. **Prepare:** inputs, environment, dependencies and configuration.
2. **Inspect:** quality checks and exploratory examination.
3. **Execute:** method, parameters and decisions.
4. **Validate:** independent checks, expected behaviour and edge cases.
5. **Interpret:** what the output does and does not establish.

Include commands, code, equations, diagrams or screenshots where they materially improve reproducibility. Pin important software and data versions.

Separate observations from interpretation. Do not imply a result was independently reproduced unless it was.

## 6. Critical examination

- What worked, and under what conditions?
- Where is the method sensitive to data, scale, parameters or assumptions?
- What are the error sources and uncertainty?
- What alternative methods would be reasonable?
- What would invalidate or materially change the conclusion?

## 7. Adaptation and application

Describe one or more ways the method could be adapted to another place, dataset, discipline or operational problem. Identify what would need to change.

## 10. Review and release

- [ ] Sources are credible, relevant and cited.
- [ ] Data and software provenance is recorded.
- [ ] Licence and redistribution conditions are respected.
- [ ] Steps are sufficiently reproducible.

- [ ] Limitations and uncertainty are visible.
- [ ] Code / notebooks / artefacts are versioned where appropriate.
- [ ] A reviewer has checked technical claims proportionate to the risk.
