# Patreon Publishing Templates

These are reusable post structures for the Spatial Methods membership experience. They are destinations and editorial formats, not promises of a fixed volume or bespoke work on demand. Adapt each post to the material actually ready for release.

---

## 1. Welcome — Start Here

**Suggested title:** Welcome to Spatial Methods

Welcome. Spatial Methods is a place to investigate questions across spatial data, methods, technologies and computation.

You do not need to follow a prescribed course or arrive with a fully formed research question. You can begin with a concept, a paper, a dataset, a tool, an application or a problem you want to understand.

**Find your next step**
- Browse the **Exploration Library** for connected topic investigations.
- Visit the **Curiosity Desk** to suggest a question or point us toward something worth examining.
- Explore the **Laboratory** for practical investigations and reproducible examples.
- Read **Research Dispatches** for evidence-led coverage of developments and open questions.

Move between understanding, research, practice, teaching and implementation as your interests change. Each exploration offers routes rather than prescribing one path.

**A useful first action:** choose one question you are genuinely curious about and follow the most useful next step—not every link at once.

## 2. Navigation — Find your way around

**Suggested title:** The Spatial Methods field guide

Use this page as a navigation point:

- **Start Here:** how the project works and how to participate.
- **Exploration Library:** structured investigations connecting questions, concepts, methods, tools and applications.
- **Curiosity Desk:** suggest questions, resources, corrections and promising directions.
- **Laboratory:** practical work, worked examples, experiments and reproducible workflows.
- **Research Dispatches:** concise analysis of research, technologies, policy, data and methods.

GitHub is the canonical home for versioned technical material, code, notebooks and corrections. Patreon provides the member-facing editorial experience and companion material. Public channels help new readers discover the work.

## 3. Curiosity Desk — Invite a question

**Suggested title:** What should we investigate next?

What have you encountered that deserves a closer look?

Send a question, paper, dataset, algorithm, tool, map, claim or real-world problem. It can be broad or incomplete. You do not need to know the right terminology.

Helpful context, if available:
- What caught your attention?
- What are you trying to understand or accomplish?
- Is there a source, example or place we should examine?
- What have you already tried?

Submissions are reviewed and prioritised by relevance, evidence, usefulness, feasibility and connection to the wider knowledge base. A submission is not a commitment to produce a dedicated investigation or provide individual consulting.

We may clarify, combine, defer or decline suggestions. Where useful, we will share the reasoning and point to existing material.

## 4. Exploration feature — Publish a question-led investigation

**Suggested title:** [Question or subject] — an exploration

**Opening:** Introduce the curiosity and why it matters. State what this exploration will and will not cover.

**The territory:** Distinguish the important branches, concepts and competing interpretations. Explain shared foundations without collapsing distinct specialisations.

**Choose a route:** Offer optional directions such as understand the concept, examine the evidence, study the method, practise with data, implement a workflow, teach the idea or apply it to a problem. Only include routes supported by the material.

**Curated resources:** Annotate selected sources with what each contributes, its level of authority, prerequisites, limitations and the best way to use it.

**Try something:** Give one bounded action with a realistic time estimate, required materials and a clear expected output.

**Where next:** Connect to related explorations and identify meaningful unanswered questions.

**Version and provenance:** Link the canonical repository material and note the review date, changes and known limitations.

## 5. Laboratory release — Practical work

**Suggested title:** Laboratory — [investigation question]

- **Question:** What are we testing or building?
- **Outcome:** What will a reader be able to inspect, reproduce or adapt?
- **Materials:** Data, software, versions, references and licence notes.
- **Method:** Link the complete reproducible workflow.
- **Evidence:** Show what was actually observed or produced.
- **Limitations:** Explain uncertainty, failure cases and what the exercise does not prove.
- **Try it:** Give the reader a bounded adaptation or verification task.
- **Continue:** Link related explorations and the canonical code or notebook.

Do not describe an investigation as reproducible until the required inputs and steps have been checked.

## 6. Research Dispatch — A timely signal

**Suggested title:** Research Dispatch — [specific signal]

**The signal:** What changed or deserves attention?

**Why it matters:** What could this mean for spatial methods, data, technology or practice?

**Evidence:** What do the strongest available sources actually establish?

**What remains open:** What is uncertain, contested or not yet demonstrated?

**Next move:** What should an interested reader inspect, test or follow?

Include source links, publication dates, confidence and review date. Distinguish reporting from interpretation.

## Editorial safeguards

- Publish only when the underlying material is ready and checked.
- Never promise an arbitrary number of full explorations each month.
- Do not promise unlimited bespoke research, private consulting or guaranteed responses.
- Keep essential public knowledge accessible where licences and project policy permit.
- Make tier differences about depth, practical support and participation—not exclusive ownership of subject areas.
- Correct errors visibly and preserve canonical version history.
