# Spatial Methods — identity and visual guide

## Name

**Spatial Methods**

Use the full name on first mention. "The collection" or "the archive" is fine after the subject is clear. Keep "spatial analysis" as the name of the discipline, not as the project name.

## Core line

*Understand the methods behind the maps, models and decisions that shape our world.*

## What this is

Spatial Methods is an open, growing collection for understanding, testing and applying the methods used to work with spatial data. It brings together explanations, research references, algorithms, implementations, exercises and learning paths.

It should feel like a thoughtful technical publication with an open door: welcoming to a curious beginner, useful to a working practitioner and rigorous enough for research and implementation.

## Voice

- **Clear:** explain the idea in familiar language before introducing specialist terms.
- **Warm:** sound like a thoughtful person helping someone understand something.
- **Confident:** say what the material does and does not establish; do not inflate claims.
- **Curious:** invite readers to test, compare, question and explore.
- **Practical:** connect methods to real tasks, places, data and decisions.
- **Rigorous underneath:** preserve mathematics, assumptions, evidence, tests, edge cases and limitations.

Prefer "Try it", "Go deeper", "Where this is useful" and "Help improve this". Avoid institutional announcements, hype, forced slang and internal project language.

## Visual direction

An editorial research identity: restrained, legible and precise. Use generous space, strong typographic hierarchy, fine rules, measured grids and diagrams that explain real relationships. The visual language should feel closer to a well-made technical journal than a software dashboard or a generic online course.

### Core palette

| Role | Colour | Hex |
|---|---|---|
| Charcoal | Primary text and dark surfaces | `#252525` |
| Warm paper | Main light background | `#F3EFE6` |
| Terracotta | Accent, links and selected marks | `#C86B4A` |
| Field grey | Secondary text and rules | `#7A817D` |
| Pale stone | Panels and quiet surfaces | `#E5E0D6` |

Use terracotta sparingly. Keep body text high-contrast. Never use colour alone to communicate status or meaning.

### Typography

- Headings: a sturdy editorial sans-serif with clear shapes.
- Body: a highly readable sans-serif at comfortable line length.
- Code, identifiers and measurements: a legible monospace.
- Use the platform's dependable system fonts unless a bundled font is deliberately added and licensed.

### Graphic language

Use coordinate grids, measured lines, intersections, contours, cells, nodes, boundaries and overlays. Diagrams should explain a method, not merely decorate a page. Avoid stock imagery of globes, generic pin icons, glowing network spheres and map screenshots used as filler.

## Mark

The mark is three identical diamond-shaped tiles arranged symmetrically in a compact triangular formation. The terracotta, charcoal and field-grey palette gives the symbol a simple visual signature without relying on a literal map pin or decorative detail. The mark is a supporting identifier, not a substitute for the written name. Use the supplied SVG as the source asset; do not redraw it inconsistently in individual documents.

## Architecture

- **Spatial Methods / Open Archive:** the public repository, with explanations, references, code, tests, notebooks, exercises, learning paths and contribution routes.
- **Spatial Methods / Guided Learning:** the Patreon learning layer, with structured study packs, worked examples, practice, workshops and discussion.

The archive remains useful without membership. Membership supports the additional teaching and community work; it does not change the licences on public repository materials.

## Naming and migration

The canonical repository is `Tessellatum-Inc/spatial-methods`. Use this URL in new references and publication materials.

The Patreon creator page is currently under the creator's personal URL. Use Spatial Methods as the publication/learning identity in its page title, description and visual assets, while retaining accurate creator attribution and account ownership.

## Publishing check

Before a page, post or graphic goes live, check:

1. Does it say what the reader will understand or be able to try?
2. Does it sound clear, warm and confident rather than promotional?
3. Are technical claims, examples and references accurate?
4. Are assumptions and limitations visible?
5. Is the visual doing explanatory work?
6. Does it distinguish the open archive from paid guided learning?
7. Are third-party assets and licences handled correctly?
