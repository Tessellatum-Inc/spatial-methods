# Spatial Methods — Curiosity Desk operating guide

**Purpose:** Turn member and community interests into a transparent, reusable editorial intake process.

## 1. What the Curiosity Desk is

The Curiosity Desk is the front door for questions, interests and possible investigations. People may submit:
- a subject or unfamiliar term;
- a question they cannot yet formulate precisely;
- a method, algorithm or technology;
- a paper, book, lecture or dataset;
- a software tool or repository;
- a practical problem or application;
- a connection they want to understand.

The Desk helps clarify the curiosity, identify useful next steps and decide whether a larger editorial response is warranted.

It is not an unlimited personal research service, private consulting channel or guarantee that every submission becomes a full exploration.

## 2. Submission experience

Keep the submission lightweight. Ask for only what helps us understand the curiosity.

Suggested prompts:
1. **What are you curious about?** A phrase, link, question, problem or rough idea is enough.
2. **What caught your attention?** Optional context.
3. **What would you like to do with it?** Optional: understand, learn, research, practise, build, teach, apply or simply explore.
4. **Anything else we should know?** Optional context, experience or constraints.

Do not force people to choose a level, profession or fixed pathway. They can be unsure.

## 3. Intake statuses

Use a small, visible status vocabulary:
- **Received:** submission has arrived.
- **Clarifying:** one important detail is needed.
- **Queued:** accepted for editorial consideration.
- **Exploring:** research or curation is underway.
- **Published:** a response or reusable resource is available.
- **Existing resource:** a strong resource already answers the need; a curated pointer is provided.
- **Not planned:** not currently in scope or feasible; explain briefly where appropriate.

A status describes editorial handling, not the value of the person's curiosity.

## 4. Triage process

### Step 1 — Understand
Restate the submitted curiosity in plain language. Identify ambiguity, synonyms and possible interpretations.

### Step 2 — Check scope
Ask whether it has a meaningful connection to spatial or computational methods. If it is adjacent, explain the connection. If it falls outside the scope, say so respectfully.

### Step 3 — Search existing work
Check the Exploration Library, GitHub archive, previous dispatches and relevant external resources before creating duplicate material.

### Step 4 — Assess priority
Use judgement across:
- relevance to Spatial Methods' domain;
- strength and availability of credible sources;
- clarity and significance of the question;
- practical or intellectual action potential;
- member/community interest;
- connections to existing explorations;
- potential to create reusable value;
- effort, expertise and verification requirements;
- freshness or time sensitivity.

Do not let popularity alone determine priority. A niche question can be highly valuable.

### Step 5 — Choose the right response
Possible outcomes:
- concise clarification or answer;
- annotated resource shelf;
- territory map;
- explanation;
- research brief;
- practical investigation or lab;
- full exploration;
- link to an existing resource with a clear explanation;
- defer or decline with a brief reason.

### Step 6 — Close the loop
Where appropriate, tell the submitter what happened and link the resulting resource. Invite corrections or a follow-on question.

## 5. Prioritisation rubric

Use a short editorial judgement, not a false-precision score. For each candidate, record:

| Dimension | Consideration |
|---|---|
| Fit | Is there a meaningful spatial/computational connection? |
| Evidence | Can we find credible sources and verify important claims? |
| Usefulness | Will the result help someone understand, decide or do something? |
| Reusability | Could the work serve people beyond the original submitter? |
| Connection | Does it strengthen or connect the existing library? |
| Feasibility | Can we produce it responsibly with available time and expertise? |
| Urgency | Is there a time-sensitive reason to prioritise it? |

Classify the decision:
- **Do now:** high usefulness and feasible.
- **Queue:** worthwhile but not urgent or capacity is limited.
- **Point to existing work:** a strong answer already exists.
- **Research further:** promising but needs clarification or evidence.
- **Decline / out of scope:** not aligned or not responsibly supportable.

## 6. Response standards

- Acknowledge the actual question; do not send generic boilerplate.
- Ask one clarifying question only when the answer would materially change the work.
- If a useful answer is already available, provide it rather than creating unnecessary content.
- Be transparent about what has and has not been researched.
- Do not imply a full exploration is underway until it is accepted and scheduled.
- Do not promise individual replies or delivery dates beyond available capacity.
- If the question is too broad, offer a useful framing rather than rejecting it for being broad.

## 7. Reusable member-facing post

### Bring a curiosity to the Spatial Methods Curiosity Desk

Have you encountered a subject, method, technology, paper, dataset or problem that you want to understand better?

Bring it here. It does not need to be a polished research question. A term you stumbled across, a link that made you curious or a problem you are trying to solve is enough.

Tell us what caught your attention and, if you know, what you hope to do with it. You might want a clear explanation, a collection of useful sources, a practical investigation, a research direction or simply a way into an unfamiliar subject.

We'll review submissions, clarify them where needed, look for useful existing resources and decide what kind of response would add the most value. Some curiosities may receive a quick curated pointer; others may become a subject map, research brief, practical lab or full exploration.

We cannot promise a bespoke course or individual research project for every submission, but we do want your questions to help shape a growing, connected knowledge environment.

**Submission prompt:** What are you curious about, and what caught your attention?

## 9. Operating boundaries

- Suggestions inform editorial priorities; they do not guarantee publication.
- The Desk is not a private consulting or technical support contract.
- Do not collect unnecessary personal or sensitive information.
- Do not publish a submitter's identifying details without permission.
- Do not reproduce submitted or third-party material without appropriate rights.
- Do not imply endorsement of a product, paper or person simply because it is featured.
- Keep a record of decisions so that submissions are not repeatedly re-triaged without reason.
