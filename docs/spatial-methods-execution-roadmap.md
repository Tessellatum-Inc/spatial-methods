# Spatial Methods — Product and execution roadmap

**Status:** Working execution plan  
**Operating principle:** Build the complete experience in connected, verifiable batches. GitHub is the canonical home for durable product, editorial and technical specifications. Patreon is the member-facing publishing and participation surface.

## 1. Product destination

Spatial Methods is a curiosity-led knowledge, research and practical capability environment. Its strong spatial and computational foundations connect outward to the sciences, technologies, data, instruments and methods through which people understand and interact with the world.

People may enter through a question, phenomenon, dataset, method, instrument, technology, application or practical problem. They may want explanation, research, teaching, software, physical experimentation or a useful product. The experience supports these different intentions without forcing everyone through one curriculum or assigning fixed learner identities.

**Core promise:** Bring a curiosity, question or problem. Spatial Methods helps you navigate the relevant knowledge, understand how it is observed and represented, investigate the methods and tools involved, and develop the capability to do something useful with it.

The organising unit remains the **curiosity / investigation**. The architecture connects foundations, observation and acquisition, measurement, data, methods, tools, experiments and applications without turning them into a mandatory sequence or rigid discipline tree. Applications range from general-purpose capabilities to domain-specific, highly specialised and frontier research systems; their distinct requirements and validation burdens must remain visible.

### Product principles

1. **Many entry points:** No assumed starting level; make prerequisites and depth visible.
2. **Different outcomes:** Explanations, study, research, teaching, practice, implementation and application are all legitimate outcomes.
3. **One subject, multiple routes:** A shared exploration can offer different routes and artefacts without becoming separate siloed products.
4. **Curated, not copied:** Find, evaluate, connect and contextualise excellent existing resources. Create original material where it adds real value.
5. **Action follows understanding:** Where appropriate, every exploration offers a practical next step, not just a reading list.
6. **Knowledge compounds:** Preserve notes, decisions, experiments, references, open questions and links to related explorations.
7. **Evidence and honesty:** State scope, prerequisites, uncertainty, source quality, licensing and limitations. Never imply completeness where it has not been achieved.
8. **Open foundations:** Keep the public technical archive independently useful. Membership pays for curation, guidance, original companion work and participation—not artificial withholding.
9. **Sustainable delivery:** Promise only what can be consistently researched, produced and maintained.
10. **Practical capability:** Connect knowledge to instruments, software, equipment, experiments and real-world systems where appropriate.
11. **Economic sustainability:** Build a diversified, evidence-led model in which valuable outputs help finance continued research, operations and development.

## 2. Experience architecture

These are connected product capabilities, not separate audience tracks.

### A. Explore — discovery and orientation
- Accept a topic, question, method, technology, paper, dataset, application or problem.
- Explain why it matters and clarify ambiguous terms.
- Map the territory: concepts, subfields, neighbouring disciplines, applications and important distinctions.
- Offer possible directions without prescribing one universal sequence.

### B. Understand — knowledge and explanation
- Provide accessible explanations alongside formal or technical depth where needed.
- Identify prerequisites and explain unfamiliar terminology.
- Link foundational ideas to advanced material.
- Distinguish established knowledge, interpretation, contested claims and open questions.

### C. Research — source discovery and evaluation
- Curate papers, books, lectures, documentation, datasets, software, repositories, experts and case studies.
- Explain why each source is useful, what it contributes and its limitations.
- Prioritise primary and authoritative sources; record provenance, dates and licences.
- Identify gaps, disagreements, variants and research questions.

### D. Practise and build — applied investigation
- Offer a first action proportionate to the subject: inspect data, reproduce a result, run a notebook, solve an exercise, compare methods or build a small artefact.
- Supply code, datasets, notebooks, diagrams, tests or worksheets only when they materially help.
- State setup requirements and verify runnable examples.
- Make outcomes and limitations observable.

### E. Teach and share — reusable knowledge
- Where useful, provide teaching notes, demonstrations, exercises, discussion prompts and adaptation guidance.
- Make reuse terms clear.
- Support members who want to explain or share what they have learned.

### F. Continue — memory and connections
- Preserve what was explored, sources consulted, actions taken, findings, surprises, unresolved questions and next steps.
- Link related investigations across subject boundaries.
- Let people resume an exploration or start another without losing the context they created.

### G. Tools and technologies — practical capability
- Connect relevant principles to instruments, sensors, machines, electronics, software and computational infrastructure.
- Explain what a tool measures, manipulates, computes or produces, how it works, and what limits it.
- Identify realistic access routes: simulation, public data, low-cost components, borrowing, hiring, shared facilities, remote infrastructure or specialist partnerships.
- Distinguish conceptual understanding, software implementation, simulation, prototype testing and physical validation.

### H. Experiments and real-world applications
- Support practical investigations where they improve understanding or capability.
- Record equipment, data, procedures, calibration, uncertainty, safety, expected outputs and limitations.
- Preserve reusable outputs such as code, datasets, protocols, models, prototypes, teaching assets and product designs.

## 3. A flexible exploration record

Create a reusable template, not a mandatory checklist that every subject must fill in.

Each exploration should capture, as applicable:

1. Curiosity, originating question and why it is interesting.
2. Scope, definitions and important distinctions.
3. Territory map: concepts, methods, subfields and applications.
4. Prerequisites and possible entry points.
5. Curated resource shelf, with annotations and source-quality notes.
6. Routes matched to different intentions (understand, research, practise, build, teach, apply or explore further).
7. A useful first action or investigation.
8. Practical artefacts and reproducibility details, where appropriate.
9. Connections to other subjects.
10. Findings, reflection, limitations and unresolved questions.
11. Suggested next steps and related explorations.
12. Provenance, licences, version and review date.

Not every exploration needs all twelve elements. The editor chooses the format that best serves the actual curiosity.

## 4. Editorial and member-facing architecture

Use five clear destinations across the member experience:

- **Start Here:** What Spatial Methods is, how to explore, how to use the archive and how to participate.
- **Exploration Library:** The growing, searchable collection of curiosity-led investigations.
- **Curiosity Desk:** A structured way for members and readers to suggest questions, interests, papers, methods, technologies or problems.
- **Laboratory:** Practical investigations, notebooks, exercises, code, datasets and worked examples.
- **Research Dispatches:** Selected developments, papers, tools, debates and emerging questions.

These are editorial destinations, not membership tiers. A single investigation may appear in several destinations through linked companion material.

### Curiosity Desk workflow

1. Receive and acknowledge a suggestion.
2. Clarify the question if needed; distinguish similarly named or easily confused subjects.
3. Assess relevance, source quality, scope, demand, action potential and reusable value.
4. Choose the right response: concise answer, annotated resource shelf, subject map, full exploration, practical lab, research brief or a clearly scoped referral to existing resources.
5. Publish reusable work to the appropriate canonical location.
6. Link it back to the suggestion and invite further questions.

A submission is input to the editorial process, not a promise of a bespoke course, private research service or guaranteed publication.

## 5. Membership and value architecture

Keep subject access broad. Do not make members choose a discipline or learning identity to gain access.

The current three-tier proposal remains provisional until checked against the creator dashboard and realistic delivery capacity:

- **Library — $4/month:** organised exploration archive, curated resource shelves, topic maps, discovery editions, member submissions and polls.
- **Laboratory — $12/month:** Library benefits plus guided practical investigations, worked examples, exercises, notebooks and technical walkthroughs where appropriate.
- **Research Circle — $29/month:** Laboratory benefits plus comparative research briefs, quarterly group workshops or clinics, project showcases, peer discussion and bounded group feedback.

Tiers distinguish depth, practical support and participation—not which subjects a person may explore. Do not promise unlimited individual support, bespoke courses on demand or weekly full explorations.

- Make every public entry point lead to a useful next action.

**Prepared asset:** `publishing/distribution-kit.md`.

**Exit condition:** A repeatable path from discovery to exploration, membership or contribution.

### Workstream 7 — Operations and sustainability
- Define editorial roles and the end-to-end workflow from intake to update.
- Estimate effort by output type and set realistic capacity limits.
- Establish a monthly discovery / investigation / practical work / connection rhythm, without requiring a full exploration every week.
- Create an editorial backlog and prioritisation rules.
- Define maintenance, review dates, corrections and archive health checks.

**Exit condition:** The system can keep operating without relying on improvisation or unsustainable publishing promises.

### Workstream 8 — Measurement and iteration
Track evidence that the experience is useful:
- Which entry points and explorations attract interest.
- Which resource links and practical activities are used.
- Which questions recur and which are resolved.
- Whether people return, continue an exploration or start another.
- Submission-to-publication conversion and editorial effort.
- Patreon conversion, tier mix, retention and participation.
- Outcomes such as completed experiments, reproduced results, projects or teaching use.

Use the evidence to improve navigation and curation before adding unnecessary features or more tiers.


### Workstream 9 — Knowledge-to-tools architecture
- Define the connected model spanning phenomena, foundations, broad observation/acquisition systems, measurement, data, methods, tools, experiments and applications.
- Represent the distinction between general-purpose, domain-specific, highly specialised and frontier/research applications without turning it into a rigid hierarchy.
- Make specialist knowledge, operating context, validation burden, safety and consequences of error visible in application records.
- Create adaptable records for tools and instruments, including operating principles, inputs/outputs, capabilities, limitations, access routes, safety and related experiments.
- Distinguish physical measurements, simulations, software implementations, prototypes and validated systems.
- Test diverse acquisition systems and applications at different specialisation levels, including environmental, medical, industrial, astronomical, underwater and computational cases; do not privilege drones or force any field into a spatial workflow.

**Canonical specification:** `docs/knowledge-tools-real-world-architecture.md`.

**Exit condition:** A person can enter through a question, tool, dataset or application and follow meaningful connections into foundations and practical work. Expensive infrastructure is not assumed to be owned.

### Workstream 10 — Economic sustainability
- Inventory existing assets, channels, offers, costs and obligations.
- Identify value created by research, publishing, software, data, education, physical tools, services and partnerships.
- Distinguish open knowledge from paid guidance, products, infrastructure and services.
- Validate customer demand and willingness to pay before building or purchasing extensively.
- Track contribution, maintenance burden, cash requirements, retention and concentration risk.
- Define reinvestment categories, reserves, asset stewardship and contributor agreements.

**Canonical specification:** `docs/economic-sustainability-architecture.md`.

**Exit condition:** Each significant activity has a clear revenue, reusable-asset or strategic-capability rationale, and the ecosystem can assess whether its offers and operations are financially sustainable.

## 8. Scheduling approach

Use milestone-based execution for the build. Use a calendar only for recurring publishing and member-facing events.

Initial sustainable rhythm, to be confirmed after estimating production effort:
- Monthly: one substantial exploration or meaningful expansion.
- Monthly: one discovery / research dispatch.
- Monthly: one practical activity or community discussion where appropriate.
- Quarterly: one Research Circle workshop or clinic, if demand and capacity support it.
- Ongoing: Curiosity Desk intake, triage and acknowledgements.

A monthly cycle may move through Discover → Investigate → Practise → Connect, but it is a production rhythm, not a promise that every subject will be completed in four weeks.

## 9. Definition of done

The initial implementation is complete only when:

- The product promise and boundaries are explicit.
- The experience supports different intentions without rigid audience silos.
- The exploration and resource templates are usable.
- Research, attribution, licensing and quality standards are documented.
- Radar demonstrates the complete experience with distinct branches and a practical first investigation.
- Patreon page copy, tiers and member-facing navigation are ready and internally consistent.
- Welcome, Curiosity Desk and first exploration posts are prepared.
- GitHub and Patreon destinations link correctly and do not contradict one another.
- The distribution and operating workflows are documented.
- The knowledge-to-tools architecture connects foundations, measurement, data, methods, instruments, experiments and applications.
- Physical, simulated and computational work are clearly distinguished.
- The economic model defines value creation, revenue options, open/paid boundaries, unit economics and reinvestment.
- Cadence and benefits are realistic for available capacity.
- All known release blockers are resolved and the final assets are verified.

**Important boundary:** GitHub documentation and prepared copy can be created and verified here. Publishing creator-page changes, setting prices, access controls and scheduling posts requires the account owner's action in Patreon unless an authorised Patreon integration becomes available. Never report these account-level changes as completed without verification.
