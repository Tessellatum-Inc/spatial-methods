## Public launch announcement

**A place to understand spatial methods**

The Spatial Methods open archive brings together explanations, algorithms, references, implementations, tests, notebooks and learning paths.

But a large archive can be difficult to navigate alone. Knowing that a method exists is different from understanding when to use it, how to test it and what its limitations mean in practice.

That is why we're opening a guided learning and research membership.

The public repository stays open and useful to everyone. Membership adds structured study packs, worked examples, exercises, solution discussions and group learning sessions.

There are three ways to take part:
- **Library:** curated notes, reading paths and monthly research digests.
- **Laboratory:** guided monthly learning packs, exercises and companion walkthroughs.
- **Research Circle:** quarterly workshops, project showcases and group critique.

Start with the open archive: https://github.com/Tessellatum-Inc/spatial-methods

Explore the membership: [Patreon page URL]

The aim is simple: make spatial methods easier to understand, practise and apply—without putting the underlying open knowledge behind a paywall.

## Patreon welcome post

# Welcome to Spatial Methods

Thank you for being here and helping build a place where spatial methods can be understood, tested and put to work.

This membership is the guided learning layer around our open technical archive. The repository remains available to everyone. Here, we organise material into study sequences, add practice and worked examples, and create space for questions and shared projects.

### Start here

1. Visit the public archive: https://github.com/Tessellatum-Inc/spatial-methods
2. Browse the learning paths and choose a topic that matches your experience.
3. Open the current monthly learning pack in the Laboratory collection.
4. Use the discussion thread to share questions, observations or a small reproducible example.
5. Tell us what you are trying to learn through the member topic poll.

### What to expect

We publish one substantial guided learning pack and one digest each month. Research Circle workshops take place quarterly. We will communicate changes clearly rather than promise a pace we cannot sustain.

### Community principles

- Be curious and generous; critique ideas, not people.
- Show assumptions, inputs and reproducible examples where possible.
- Respect privacy and do not post confidential client or personal data.
- Attribute sources and respect licences.
- Distinguish a teaching example from production-ready software.
- Share corrections openly so the canonical public archive can improve.

Thank you for supporting open technical knowledge and helping turn it into practical capability.

## First member release announcement

# Laboratory 01 is ready: Do two line segments meet?

Our first guided learning pack explores segment intersection: the deceptively rich problem of deciding whether two finite lines meet, touch or overlap.

The pack connects the geometry to an exact orientation predicate, then works through proper crossings, endpoint contacts, collinear overlap and disjoint cases. It includes five exercises, hints, worked answers and a production-readiness discussion.

Start with the public explanation and tested code:
https://github.com/Tessellatum-Inc/spatial-methods/tree/main/algorithms/geometry

Then open the member learning pack and work through the exercises before reading the answers.

Bring your questions to this month's discussion thread. Please use small synthetic examples rather than confidential project data.
