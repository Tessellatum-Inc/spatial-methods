# Spatial Methods — Product brief

**Version:** 0.1  
**Status:** Foundation specification  
**Product category:** Curiosity-led knowledge, research, technology and practical capability environment

## 1. Product in one sentence

Spatial Methods helps people investigate the spatial, computational and connected scientific-technological world by turning a curiosity, question or problem into navigable knowledge, useful next actions, practical capability and connections they can continue to build on.

## 2. The problem

The difficulty is rarely finding information. The difficulty is doing the work required to make information useful.

A person following an unfamiliar subject often has to become their own:
- researcher, to find credible and relevant sources;
- librarian, to organise and annotate them;
- curriculum designer, to decide what to understand first;
- technical guide, to distinguish concepts, methods and tools;
- project manager, to decide what to do next;
- editor, to connect discoveries and preserve what they learned.

Search engines return documents, platforms return courses, repositories return code and papers return specialised findings. These are valuable, but the person still has to assemble a coherent journey across them.

Spatial Methods reduces that coordination burden. It does not claim to replace original sources, formal education, expert judgement or the work of research. It makes the landscape easier to enter, assess, navigate and act within.

## 3. Who it serves — expressed as intentions

Do not treat these as exclusive personas, membership segments or fixed learning levels. One person may move between several intentions during one investigation.

| Intention | The person is trying to… | Useful support may include… |
|---|---|---|
| Understand | Make sense of a concept, term, method or phenomenon | Clear explanations, diagrams, examples, definitions and prerequisites |
| Learn | Build knowledge progressively and check their understanding | Sequenced resources, exercises, reflection and suggested next steps |
| Research | Understand the state of knowledge or investigate a question | Literature maps, source evaluation, methodological comparisons, evidence gaps and open questions |
| Practise | Gain familiarity by doing | Exercises, datasets, guided activities, notebooks and worked examples |
| Build | Implement or adapt a method or system | Specifications, code, tests, architecture notes and reproducible workflows |
| Teach | Explain a subject or support someone else's learning | Teaching notes, demonstrations, activities, assessment prompts and reuse guidance |
| Apply | Make a decision or solve a real problem | Case studies, selection criteria, assumptions, trade-offs and applied workflows |
| Explore | Follow an interest without a predetermined deliverable | Territory maps, surprising connections, related questions and optional directions |

The platform should let someone choose an intention when it helps, but must not require them to declare an identity or follow a prescribed route.

## 4. Scope

### Core domain

Spatial and computational methods remain important roots, but the intended territory is broader. Spatial Methods connects the foundations, data, methods, technologies and tools that help people understand and interact with the world. Relevant territories may include:
- mathematics, statistics, geometry, topology and information theory;
- physics, chemistry, biology and other sciences where they connect to investigation and technology;
- measurement, sensing, signals, imaging and experimental methods;
- spatial data, geographic information, remote sensing and geospatial systems;
- algorithms, software, data engineering, AI and computational infrastructure;
- engineering, electronics, robotics, automation and scientific instrumentation;
- simulation, modelling, optimisation and operations research;
- applications in research, industry, environment, infrastructure, health and human systems.

This is not a claim to cover every subject exhaustively. A curiosity should be followed into the fields that genuinely explain it. Do not force unrelated subjects into a spatial workflow merely because Spatial Methods has spatial roots.

### What belongs

A subject belongs when it meaningfully helps explain a phenomenon, measure or represent a system, generate or interpret data, develop a method or technology, operate or build a tool, conduct an experiment, or solve a real-world problem. Some subjects are worth exploring for foundational or intellectual reasons even when no immediate application is known.

A curiosity can enter through a phenomenon, question, application, technology, instrument, paper, dataset, algorithm, tool or practical problem. The entry point does not dictate the eventual structure.

### What does not belong by default

- Unfocused coverage that has no meaningful connection to the question, investigation or wider knowledge architecture.
- Unannotated link dumps or automated collections presented as curation.
- Unsupported claims of comprehensive coverage.
- Repackaging copyrighted courses, papers, books, figures or datasets without permission.
- Guaranteed individual supervision, consulting or custom project delivery as an implied membership benefit.

## 4A. The four-pillar structure

The permanent organising structure of Spatial Methods has four pillars:

1. **Spatial Algorithms** — reusable computational methods, their theory, implementations, assumptions, tests and limitations.
2. **Spatial Recipes** — reproducible workflows that combine data, algorithms, tools, parameters and decisions to accomplish a task.
3. **Spatial Data Collection** — the broad systems and methods used to observe, measure, sample, generate, acquire and transmit spatial information, from field and laboratory instruments to satellites, industrial systems and digital records.
4. **Theoretical Foundations** — the mathematics, statistics, spatial science, physical sciences, life sciences, computer science and engineering principles that explain the other pillars.

These are the four top-level pillars, not four mandatory stages. People can enter through any of them and move between them in any direction.

Applications are contexts in which the pillars are combined, not a fifth pillar. Applications may be general-purpose, domain-specific, highly specialised or frontier/research-oriented. The required depth, expertise and validation must match the intended use.

Data, instruments, software, experiments, teaching, research and products are resources or outputs connected across the pillars; they do not create additional top-level categories. The detailed definitions and relationship model are maintained in [the four-pillar architecture](knowledge-tools-real-world-architecture.md).

## 5. Product promise

Bring a question, interest or problem. Spatial Methods helps you:
1. understand what the subject includes and what it does not;
2. find credible sources and understand why they matter;
3. see how concepts, methods, disciplines and applications connect;
4. choose an entry point and a useful next action;
5. investigate, practise, research, teach, build or apply according to your purpose;
6. preserve discoveries and continue without starting from zero.

The promise is navigation, curation, interpretation and useful action—not guaranteed mastery, a prescribed qualification or a complete answer to every possible question.

## 6. What makes the experience distinct

### Curiosity is the entry point
The user does not need to know the correct academic term, discipline or job title before beginning. We help clarify the question and reveal the relevant vocabulary.

### Different intentions can coexist
An exploration can serve a person who wants an accessible explanation and another who needs formal methods, literature or implementation detail. We expose the appropriate routes without forcing everyone through every layer.

### Curation includes judgement
A useful resource shelf says what a source contributes, who or what it helps, what it assumes, how current it is and where its limitations lie. A list of links alone is not enough.

### Understanding connects to action
Where the subject permits, the user should be able to do something: inspect a dataset, reproduce an analysis, compare methods, solve a problem, run code, teach a concept, use an instrument, build a prototype or formulate a research question. Practical work may involve software, simulation, physical equipment, shared laboratories or remote infrastructure.

### Knowledge accumulates
The result of an exploration should be reusable and connected to previous and future work. It should not disappear into a one-off post.

## 7. Product experience capabilities

1. **Orient:** clarify the curiosity, terms, scope and possible interpretations.
2. **Map:** reveal the territory, distinctions, foundations, branches and applications.
3. **Curate:** discover, assess, annotate and connect high-quality resources.
4. **Explain:** make ideas understandable at appropriate depths.
5. **Route:** offer optional paths based on the person's current intention.
6. **Activate:** suggest a meaningful first action or practical investigation.
7. **Preserve:** record findings, sources, experiments, open questions and next steps.
8. **Connect:** link the exploration to related subjects and other investigations.
9. **Improve:** correct, update and expand the resource as evidence and community contributions arrive.

## 8. Trust and quality commitments

- Prefer primary literature, official documentation, recognised institutions and well-maintained technical sources.
- Distinguish source facts from editorial interpretation and hypotheses.
- Record publication or update dates when freshness matters.
- Explain why a source is included; note paywalls, prerequisites, access limitations and known caveats.
- Respect copyright, licences, attribution and data-use restrictions.
- Verify code, calculations and procedures before calling them tested or reproducible.
- State uncertainty, assumptions, scope and limitations.
- Correct mistakes visibly and update derivative materials when canonical material changes.
- Do not confuse popularity, search ranking or AI-generated summaries with evidence of quality.

## 9. Business and publishing model

### Public archive
GitHub holds canonical technical explanations, code, tests, notebooks, research notes, references, corrections and version history. It should remain independently useful.

### Patreon
Patreon is the membership, guided experience and participation surface: curated navigation, companion explanations, practical guidance, research dispatches, discussions and bounded group activities.

### Other publishing channels
Newsletter, Medium, LinkedIn, talks and other channels help people discover useful ideas and enter an exploration. They should link to canonical resources rather than create competing versions.

### Membership principle
People should not have to choose a subject or identity-based tier. Membership differences may reflect the amount of guided support, practical material and participation available. Exact prices and benefits remain subject to capacity and creator-dashboard verification.

## 10. Economic sustainability

Spatial Methods should progressively fund its own operations and growth. Its economic model should support the intellectual mission without putting every piece of knowledge behind a paywall or allowing commercial incentives to distort research quality.

Value may be created through memberships, publishing, education, software, data products, APIs, physical tools, experiment kits, technical services, commissioned research, sponsorships, grants and institutional partnerships. These are possible routes, not assumed revenue. Validate demand, pricing, rights, delivery costs and maintenance before scaling.

Keep the public technical archive independently useful. Paid value should come from genuine additional guidance, participation, products, infrastructure or services. Track contribution after direct costs, delivery effort, recurring obligations, cash requirements and customer concentration. Reinvest deliberately in operations, research, equipment, product development, contributors and reserves.

Treat reusable research, software, datasets, protocols, prototypes, teaching resources, distribution and partnerships as assets. Record ownership, licences, provenance, maintenance and contributor terms. Prefer low fixed costs and borrow, rent, simulate or partner for expensive infrastructure until demand justifies ownership.

The detailed operating model is maintained in [Economic sustainability architecture](economic-sustainability-architecture.md).

## 11. What success means

The product is working when people can:
- arrive with an imprecise curiosity and find a credible way in;
- understand why selected resources are relevant;
- choose a route that matches what they want to accomplish;
- take a useful next action;
- return to continue or start a connected exploration;
- reuse the work in study, research, teaching, development or professional practice.

Measure usefulness and continuity, not simply the number of posts or links published.

## 12. Product boundaries

Spatial Methods is not:
- a universal search engine or exhaustive index of the internet;
- a replacement for universities, formal qualifications, expert supervision or peer review;
- a promise that every submitted curiosity receives a full bespoke exploration;
- a repository that republishes third-party material wholesale;
- a single fixed curriculum through which everyone must progress;
- a guarantee that an example or teaching implementation is suitable for production.

## 13. Decision test

Before creating a feature, post or resource, ask:

1. What real curiosity, question or task does this serve?
2. Which intention(s) might it support?
3. What does it help someone understand, decide or do?
4. Why is this source or artefact worth including?
5. What is the next useful action?
6. How will this connect to or improve the growing knowledge environment?
7. Can we deliver and maintain it honestly?

If these questions have no useful answers, do not create the asset merely to fill a publishing calendar.

For physical and technical work, also ask whether the proposed activity is safe, accessible, reproducible and honest about the difference between simulation, prototype and validated operation.
