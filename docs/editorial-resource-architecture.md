# Spatial Methods — Editorial and resource curation standard

**Version:** 0.2  
**Related specifications:** [Product brief](product-brief.md), [Experience architecture](experience-architecture.md), [Execution roadmap](spatial-methods-execution-roadmap.md).

## 1. Editorial organising principle

Organise the experience around **curiosities and investigations**, not a fixed academic hierarchy, job-title catalogue or universal syllabus.

A curiosity may begin with a subject, question, method, technology, paper, dataset, application, problem or unfamiliar term. The editor clarifies the entry point, maps the relevant territory and creates useful routes for different intentions.

Subject and method metadata remain important for search and connections, but they are not the sole structure of the experience.

## 2. Different intentions, shared exploration

An exploration may support one or several of these intentions:
- understand;
- learn;
- research;
- practise;
- build;
- teach;
- apply;
- explore further.

These are optional ways of using the material, not learner identities or fixed levels. Do not create separate silos for students, PhD researchers, professionals or educators. Make prerequisites and depth explicit within the relevant resource.

## 3. Editorial formats

Choose the smallest format that genuinely answers the need.

| Format | Use when… | Minimum useful content |
|---|---|---|
| Orientation | A term or question needs clarification | Meaning, scope, distinctions and useful next direction |
| Resource shelf | Strong existing sources already do most of the teaching | Annotated, evaluated resources and suggested order or routes |
| Territory map | The subject is broad, branched or confusing | Main concepts, branches, connections and distinctions |
| Explanation | A concept needs a clear original explanation | Definitions, reasoning, example and limitations |
| Research brief | Evidence, methods or developments need synthesis | Question, sources, findings, disagreement and open questions |
| Practical investigation | Doing something materially improves understanding | Goal, prerequisites, steps, expected output and verification |
| Teaching companion | Someone needs to explain or teach the subject | Learning outcomes, demonstration, activity and reuse notes |
| Full exploration | Several formats must be connected to serve the curiosity | A tailored combination of the above, plus continuity and related doors |

Do not inflate every curiosity into a full exploration. A concise, high-quality answer can be the correct product.

## 4. Resource curation standard

A resource is not curated merely because it has been found or linked.

For each significant resource, record as appropriate:
1. **Identity:** title, creator or issuing organisation, URL or canonical location.
2. **Type:** paper, book, lecture, documentation, dataset, software, repository, standard, case study, expert or other.
3. **Contribution:** what it helps the reader understand or do.
4. **Relevance:** which question, route or concept it supports.
5. **Authority and provenance:** why the source is credible for this claim or task.
6. **Prerequisites:** assumed knowledge, tools or access.
7. **Currency:** publication, release or last-checked date where freshness matters.
8. **Limitations:** scope, caveats, known omissions, paywall or access restrictions.
9. **Reuse:** licence, attribution requirements and restrictions where applicable.
10. **Editorial judgement:** why this resource was selected over alternatives.

Not every field applies to every resource. Never invent missing metadata.

### Source-quality considerations

Evaluate sources in context:
- Primary research is valuable for original findings, but may be narrow, contested or difficult to interpret.
- Review articles and textbooks can provide synthesis, but may lag fast-moving fields.
- Official documentation is authoritative about a product's stated behaviour, not automatically about broader scientific claims.
- Software repositories reveal implementation and maintenance evidence, but code presence alone does not prove correctness.
- Datasets need provenance, coverage, temporal and spatial resolution, known biases, licence and fitness-for-purpose checks.
- Lectures and tutorials can be excellent introductions; distinguish instruction from primary evidence.
- Expert commentary can provide insight, but identify the basis and limits of the claim.

Do not collapse these considerations into a universal numeric trust score. Explain the judgement.

## 5. Research workflow

1. Translate the initial curiosity into one or more answerable questions.
2. Identify terminology, synonyms and easily confused concepts.
3. Search primary and authoritative sources before relying on derivative summaries.
4. Follow references and citations to foundational work where useful.
5. Compare sources, methods, dates and claims.
6. Record disagreements, uncertainties and evidence gaps.
7. Test claims, code or procedures when the output depends on them.
8. Annotate selected resources for a specific purpose.
9. Cite sources close to the claims they support.
10. Set a review date for time-sensitive material.

Use web search, repositories, academic indexes, standards, institutional libraries and domain-specific catalogues as appropriate. Search broadly, curate selectively.

## 6. Original content standard

Create original material when it adds something the external source does not provide in the needed context:
- a clearer explanation;
- a comparison or synthesis;
- a connection across disciplines;
- a worked example or reproducible implementation;
- a practical investigation;
- a teaching adaptation;
- an explanation of limitations or common misconceptions;
- a route through several otherwise disconnected resources.

Do not rewrite a source merely to make it look like a Spatial Methods asset. Attribute ideas, quote sparingly and respect copyright.

## 7. Technical and research quality

For technical content, state applicable:
- problem definition and intended use;
- notation, units and coordinate reference systems;
- assumptions and data requirements;
- algorithm or method and relevant variants;
- correctness considerations;
- complexity and performance assumptions;
- numerical precision and edge cases;
- limitations, failure modes and alternatives;
- software versions, environment and reproducibility instructions.

For research synthesis:
- distinguish established findings from hypotheses and editorial interpretation;
- represent competing evidence fairly;
- state search and selection limitations when relevant;
- avoid claiming novelty or exhaustive coverage without a defensible basis;
- identify unanswered questions rather than filling gaps with speculation.

## 8. Practical activity standard

A practical activity should identify:
- the question or capability being investigated;
- prerequisites and expected effort, if known;
- tools, data and access requirements;
- ordered instructions;
- expected output or observation;
- checks for interpreting the result;
- likely failure modes and troubleshooting;
- what the activity does not demonstrate;
- a next step or extension.

A practical activity is not automatically required. Use one only when it improves the experience.

## 9. Cross-disciplinary connections

Connections should explain the relationship:
- shared mathematical or computational foundation;
- one field providing data or measurement for another;
- one method adapting or extending another;
- contrasting assumptions or models;
- a common application with different approaches;
- a dependency or prerequisite.

Avoid arbitrary association lists. Every connection should help someone understand why following it may be useful.

## 10. Publication and maintenance

- GitHub is the canonical location for durable technical material, code, tests, notebooks, references and corrections.
- Patreon provides editorial navigation, companion guidance, practical framing, discussion and membership participation.
- Public publishing channels provide discovery and link to canonical resources.
- Downloadable assets include version, provenance and reuse information.
- When a canonical resource changes, review linked Patreon and derivative materials.
- Mark resources that need review; do not silently present stale material as current.
- Correct errors visibly and preserve useful change history.

## 11. Editorial decision test

Before publishing, answer:
1. What curiosity or user task does this serve?
2. Why is this the right format?
3. What has been evaluated rather than merely collected?
4. What can the reader understand or do after using it?
5. What are the important caveats?
6. How does it connect to the wider library?
7. Can it be maintained responsibly?

If the work does not improve understanding, navigation, evidence, action or continuity, do not publish it simply to meet a quota.
