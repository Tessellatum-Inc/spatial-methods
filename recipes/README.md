# Spatial Recipes

Spatial Recipes are reproducible workflows that combine data, algorithms, tools, measurements and decisions to accomplish a defined task.

A recipe is not merely a sequence of software clicks. It should explain its purpose, assumptions, inputs, choices, quality controls, outputs, limitations and the evidence needed to trust the result.

## Recipe record principles

Each recipe should, as applicable:

1. State the problem, intended users, application context and fitness-for-purpose requirements.
2. Identify required data, instruments, software, skills, permissions and safety considerations.
3. Explain the relevant theory and why the selected methods are appropriate.
4. Specify the workflow, decision points, parameters and alternatives.
5. Preserve raw inputs, provenance, metadata and reproducibility information.
6. Define independent checks, acceptance criteria and failure handling before execution.
7. Distinguish desk research, synthetic demonstration, public-data processing, prototype testing and physical validation.
8. State limitations, maintenance needs and possible adaptations.

Recipes may link to many algorithms, foundations and collection methods. They do not replace those canonical records; they connect them into useful work.
