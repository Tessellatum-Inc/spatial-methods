# Nairobi pilot — stakeholder enquiry templates

**Status:** Draft correspondence for review. Nothing in this document has been sent.  
**Project:** Proposed non-intrusive public-transport stop and roadside-asset inventory, Nairobi.  
**Candidate only:** Ngong Road, Adams Arcade–Prestige Mall. No site or field activity is approved.

Use these messages as starting points. Personalise the sender details, attach only the short project brief if requested, and retain written responses in the project evidence register. Do not imply that any organisation has agreed to participate.

## 1. KURA Nairobi Region — road authority and access

**To:** nairobi@kura.go.ke  
**Subject:** Enquiry: proposed non-intrusive spatial inventory along Ngong Road, Nairobi

Dear KURA Nairobi Regional Office,

I am developing a small applied geospatial research and learning project through Spatial Methods, focused on documenting apparent public-transport stopping locations and selected visible roadside assets along a short urban corridor in Nairobi.

We are assessing the Adams Arcade–Prestige Mall section of Ngong Road as a possible pilot area. This is a feasibility enquiry only; no fieldwork has commenced and no equipment will be deployed without the relevant approvals.

Could you please advise:

1. Whether the exact proposed section is under KURA's management.
2. Whether a non-intrusive inventory conducted solely from lawful public pedestrian areas requires formal permission, a no-objection letter or another authorisation.
3. Any site-specific safety requirements, ongoing works or restrictions.
4. The appropriate office or officer to review a short project scope and proposed study-area map.

The proposed activity is limited to documenting visible stopping locations and selected roadside features. It excludes traffic counts, passenger interviews, intrusive works, road-reserve boundary surveys and changes to transport operations.

Thank you for directing us to the appropriate process.

Kind regards,

Spatial Methods

## 2. Measurement Systems Limited — equipment capability enquiry

**To:** sales@measurementsystems.org  
**Subject:** Technical enquiry: survey equipment hire and supervised demonstration for a small Nairobi pilot

Dear Measurement Systems Team,

I am preparing a small applied geospatial pilot in Nairobi through Spatial Methods. The proposed task is a limited inventory of apparent public-transport stopping locations and selected visible roadside assets. We are currently at the feasibility stage and have not selected a site or scheduled field acquisition.

I would appreciate technical guidance on equipment access and the practical options for a small project:

1. Which GNSS RTK, total station, digital/optical level or other systems would be suitable for a short urban asset-inventory task, and under what site conditions would you recommend against each?
2. Do you offer short-term hire, a supervised demonstration or introductory operator training? Please share minimum hire periods and indicative rates.
3. What calibration certificates, verification records, equipment identifiers and configuration details are supplied with a hire?
4. Is an experienced operator or technical support available, and what would that cost?
5. What accessories, correction services, connectivity and site prerequisites would be needed?
6. Could you provide a quotation for a modest, one-day familiarisation or demonstration before we decide whether a field pilot is feasible?

We are not seeking to purchase equipment at this stage. Our priority is to understand the actual measurement capability, documentation and total cost required before defining a method.

Thank you for your time.

Kind regards,

Spatial Methods

## 3. University of Nairobi — Geospatial and Space Technology

**To:** surveying@uonbi.ac.ke  
**Subject:** Request for technical discussion: applied spatial data collection and quality-assessment pilot

Dear Department of Geospatial and Space Technology,

I am developing a practical capstone project through Spatial Methods, intended to connect measurement science, spatial data collection, algorithms and reproducible workflows to a bounded real-world application.

The current pilot concept is a small inventory of apparent public-transport stopping locations and selected visible roadside assets along a short Nairobi corridor. The work is still at desk-feasibility stage; no site has been approved and no field observations have been collected.

I would welcome the opportunity to ask whether the Department could point me toward an appropriate academic or technical contact for a short discussion on:

- selecting a fit-for-purpose acquisition method for an urban roadside environment;
- establishing suitable reference information and genuinely independent positional checks;
- defining realistic quality requirements and limitations;
- possible student/research collaboration, supervised learning or technical review, if appropriate.

I am not assuming a formal partnership or requesting endorsement. At this stage, I am seeking methodological guidance and would be glad to share a concise project definition in advance.

Thank you for considering the enquiry.

Kind regards,

Spatial Methods

## 6. Sending and response protocol

1. Review the message and insert the actual sender's name and contact details.
2. Send the KURA enquiry first to establish road authority and access requirements.
3. Send the equipment and university enquiries in parallel; these are capability discussions and do not authorise fieldwork.
4. Contact the County for stop-designation and transport-data guidance, using a verified official channel.
5. Log date, recipient, exact message, attachments, response, follow-up date and any conditions.
6. Treat silence as no approval. Verbal guidance should be recorded and, where it affects access or safety, confirmed in writing.
7. Do not schedule a field visit or collect measurements until the relevant readiness gates in the [reconnaissance pack](reconnaissance-readiness-pack.md) are met.
