# Nairobi pilot feasibility assessment

**Status:** Desk assessment and outreach plan; no site selected, permissions obtained, equipment reserved or fieldwork conducted.  
**Decision:** Conditional proceed to reconnaissance and capability enquiries only. **Do not mobilise a field team yet.**

## 1. Decision summary

The proposed small public-transport stop and roadside-asset inventory is plausible as a first Spatial Methods field capstone, but it is not yet field-ready.

The desk review identifies:
- two corridor candidates with a documented public-transport context;
- local equipment suppliers that advertise relevant instruments, hire and/or technical support;
- potential academic and technical institutions for a conversation about supervision or collaboration;
- an official route to verify licensed survey professionals;
- clear road-authority and safety questions that must be resolved before field activity.

The most important practical conclusion is that **we do not need to purchase a total station or GNSS receiver to test feasibility**. First ask suppliers and potential partners about short-term hire, demonstration access, training, operator support and reference/check capability. No current stock, rental price, instrument condition or availability has been confirmed.

## 2. Candidate corridor shortlist

The corridor is not selected. The two options below are reconnaissance candidates, not approved work sites.

### Candidate A — Ngong Road: Adams Arcade to Prestige Mall

**Why it is on the shortlist**
- Kenya Gazette Notice No. 16879, published 20 December 2024, lists a gazetted core public-transport route that includes Ngong Road, Adams Arcade and Prestige Mall among its key stages.
- The bounded segment offers a clear, recognisable study extent and a transport context tied to an official route notice.
- It could support an inventory of stop locations, visible stop infrastructure and pedestrian-interface features.

**Risks and unknowns**
- Ngong Road is a major traffic corridor. Safe observation positions, pedestrian access, crossing arrangements and equipment setup must be assessed.
- The Gazette route notice does not establish the current operating status, exact boarding position or legal designation of every observed stopping point.
- Road ownership/classification and the relevant road authority for the exact segment must be confirmed.
- A roadside inventory must not obstruct passengers, pedestrians, operators or traffic.

**Initial assessment:** Best first desk candidate because the route context has an official gazetted source. It is not automatically the safest or easiest field site.

### Candidate B — Ngara Road / Forest Road corridor

**Why it is on the shortlist**
- Published route references describe services linking the CBD, Ngara, Pangani and northern suburbs.
- It may provide a varied urban environment and a useful range of roadside and pedestrian-interface conditions.

**Risks and unknowns**
- The route guide is secondary information, not proof of current route operation or officially designated stops.
- Exact study limits and stop locations need confirmation.
- Traffic, roadside activity, pedestrian space and safe observation positions require reconnaissance.
- Road ownership/classification and permission route must be established for the exact segment.

**Initial assessment:** Useful alternative if the first corridor proves too exposed or logistically difficult, but it needs stronger route and site verification.

### Corridor selection rule

Do not score or select a corridor from desk maps alone. During reconnaissance, assess:
1. Safe, continuous pedestrian access and places to stand without entering the carriageway.
2. Number and diversity of observable features within a compact area.
3. Whether stops and assets can be observed without interfering with passengers or operators.
4. Whether a responsible authority or property manager can clarify access and permission.
5. Availability of a suitable independent reference method and safe setup locations.
6. Transport, time, team size, weather exposure and practical cost.
7. Whether the site avoids sensitive facilities, security restrictions and unnecessary personal-data collection.

If neither corridor meets the safety and access criteria, choose a smaller controlled site or defer the pilot. Do not relax safety requirements to preserve the original idea.

## 4. Independent professional review and reference capability

For the positional verification stage, identify a competent person who can:
- review the measurand and measurement plan;
- advise on suitable control/reference methods;
- assess whether the reference uncertainty is adequate for the proposed acceptance decision;
- verify equipment and field procedures within their competence;
- conduct or supervise genuinely independent check observations;
- review the final quality statement.

Where the work falls within regulated land-surveying practice, verify the professional's current status directly through the [Land Surveyors' Board register](https://www.lsb.go.ke/register). The register distinguishes active and non-active practising status and lists approved assistants separately. An assistant is not a substitute for the responsible licensed surveyor.

Do not assume that a supplier's technician, a training instructor, a GIS analyst or the person who collected the original coordinates is automatically an independent reference authority. The role, competence, independence and responsibility must be explicit.

Potential route:
1. Ask UoN GST or KISM for a referral to an appropriate measurement/surveying professional.
2. Verify any proposed licensed surveyor and current practising status through the LSB register where applicable.
3. Agree a limited scope: method review and/or independent check observations, with deliverables and fees defined.
4. Keep the reference observations separate from the production observations and adjustment.

## 5. Authority, access and legal boundaries

Before any field activity, identify who controls the exact road segment and the space where observations would take place.

Potential public bodies to contact for direction:
- Nairobi City County — county transport, roads and public-transport functions.
- Kenya Urban Roads Authority (KURA) — where the road is under its mandate.
- Kenya National Highways Authority (KeNHA) — where the segment is a national trunk road.
- Other relevant property/land managers if the proposed observation area is not public county space.

Do not infer the road authority from the road name. Confirm the road classification and managing authority for the selected segment using authoritative records and written clarification.

The Nairobi City County Transport Act, 2020 defines bus stops in terms of designated spaces and contains provisions concerning county roads and road reserves. The County Transport Regulations also address road-reserve boundaries and encroachment. This pilot must not move, mark, occupy, obstruct or alter any road asset or road reserve. A passive inventory from a safe, publicly accessible position is not permission to set up survey equipment or conduct a formal survey.

The [Kenya Roads Board map portal](https://maps.krb.go.ke/kenya-roads-board12769/maps) provides road-network classification and inventory context, but its own disclaimer says its maps are not authoritative for boundary delimitation. Confirm the controlling authority with the relevant agency.

Relevant official starting points:
- [Nairobi City County Transport Act, 2020](https://nairobiassembly.go.ke/ncca/wp-content/uploads/act/2021/Nairobi-City-County-Transport-Act-2020.pdf)
- [Nairobi City County Transport Regulations](https://nairobi.go.ke/wp-content/uploads/NCC-Transport-Act-Regulations-23.01.2025-final.pdf)
- [Ministry of Roads and Transport — State Department for Transport](https://www.transport.go.ke/background-sdt)
- [KURA](https://kura.go.ke/)
- [KeNHA permit information](https://kenha.co.ke/faqs/)
- [Land Surveyors' Board](https://www.lsb.go.ke/)

A written enquiry should describe the activity accurately: a small educational/research inventory of visible public-transport stop and roadside features, with no excavation, no installation, no road closure, no interference with traffic or passengers, no boundary determination and no claim of official designation. Ask whether written permission, notification, escort or a specific fieldwork protocol is required.

## 6. Proposed minimum pilot design

Keep the first exercise small enough to manage safely and rigorously.

**Provisional scope**
- One short corridor, selected only after reconnaissance and authority confirmation.
- A defined set of visible public-transport stopping locations and selected roadside assets.
- Point inventory and attributes; no road-reserve boundary mapping.
- No traffic counts, passenger interviews, personal-data collection or service-performance claims.
- No drone operations or intrusive equipment setup in the initial pilot.
- No claim that an observed stopping location is officially designated unless confirmed by an authoritative source.

**Feature classes**
1. Observed stopping location — distinguish verified designation from observed use and uncertain status.
2. Stop infrastructure — sign, shelter, seating, lighting and other visible features.
3. Pedestrian interface — footway, kerb/ramp, crossing, obstruction and surface condition.
4. Optional independent reference/check features, only if the method and permission are approved.

**Observation states**
- Present and observed.
- Not present after adequate inspection.
- Not assessed.
- Inaccessible.
- Obscured.
- Uncertain.

Never convert an inaccessible or obscured feature into an assumed absence.

## 7. Minimum field team and operating model

The pilot should not be a solo exercise in a live traffic environment.

Minimum roles:
- Field lead responsible for scope, safety and records.
- Measurement operator competent with the selected equipment.
- Independent reviewer/check operator who does not use the production observations to tune the result.
- Additional observer/data recorder where the environment or method requires it.

One person may hold more than one role only where competence and independence are not compromised. The independent check function must remain independent in practice, not just in the project chart.

Before mobilisation, complete:
- site-specific risk assessment and safe working method;
- access and permission confirmation;
- team/role and emergency contact plan;
- equipment and pre-use verification register;
- field forms, data dictionary and privacy controls;
- reference/check design and acceptance rules;
- weather, visibility and operational stop-work criteria.

## 9. Go/no-go rule

**Proceed to reconnaissance and enquiries:** Yes.

**Proceed to field acquisition:** No, not yet.

A field go decision requires all of the following:
- one site and bounded study extent selected;
- safe access and relevant permissions resolved;
- road authority identified;
- method and equipment selected from the actual measurand and environment;
- competent operator and independent reviewer identified;
- reference and acceptance plan approved before acquisition;
- data/privacy and safety arrangements approved;
- practical costs and logistics accepted.

If a single critical dependency remains unresolved, keep the work at desk design or conduct only a permitted non-instrument reconnaissance.
