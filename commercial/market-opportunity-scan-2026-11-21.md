# Tessellatum zero-budget revenue opportunity scan

**Date:** 2026-11-21  
**Status:** initial market signals; no customer commitments or sales claimed  
**Operating constraint:** no spend before revenue; no paid subscriptions, advertising, or external commitments without approval.

## Evidence observed

A live scan of public geospatial freelance listings on 21 November 2026 surfaced demand signals for:
- interactive web maps and geospatial web application development;
- georeferencing and GIS data preparation;
- satellite-image processing and remote-sensing outputs;

Examples included a $1,500 fixed-price geospatial web/UI mapping listing, an $800 georeferencing project, a $400 satellite-image visual production task, and a $6,000 GIS training listing. These are individual advertised budgets, not typical rates, guaranteed demand, or evidence of Tessellatum's eligibility. Source pages can change or close.

## Best near-term commercial direction

Do not start by building another general-purpose platform. Package narrowly scoped outcomes that buyers already request and that can be delivered with existing open-source tools.

### Offer A — Spatial Data Rescue & Readiness

**Buyer pain:** spatial files are messy, inconsistent, misprojected, incomplete, or unusable in downstream analysis.

**Deliverables:** input inventory; CRS/geometry/schema checks; repair log; clean GeoPackage/GeoJSON/CSV outputs; reproducible processing script; concise QA report.

**Why first:** bounded scope, low infrastructure needs, reusable automation, and clear before/after value.

### Offer B — Satellite Evidence Pack

**Buyer pain:** a client needs a defensible, time-bounded view of land cover, site conditions, environmental change or exposure, but does not need a full enterprise platform.

**Deliverables:** documented imagery sources and dates; selected AOI chips/composites; selected indices; limitations and cloud/valid-pixel notes; map/report; reproducibility manifest.

**Guardrail:** distinguish remote-sensing indicators from validated classifications. No claims of field truth without field data.

### Offer C — Interactive Spatial Prototype

**Buyer pain:** a team needs a working map or spatial workflow to test a service, communicate evidence or validate a product idea.

**Deliverables:** focused web map, data layer integration, one or two workflows, deployment handover and short technical note.

**Guardrail:** fixed scope, no open-ended bespoke platform commitments, and hosting costs disclosed separately.

## Recommended first commercial experiment

Lead with Offer A, because it is easiest to scope, demonstrate and deliver without buying imagery or hosting. Create one public, synthetic/sample-data demonstration and a one-page service specification. In parallel, use the Fiji project as a technical case study of reproducible evidence and QA, while respecting source-data and imagery licences and avoiding publication of blinded labels.

Do not claim these offers are already validated. The next validation step is to compare 10–15 current buyer requests, identify repeated deliverables and price ranges, then prepare a small portfolio and outreach drafts. Sending messages, submitting proposals, accepting contracts, and quoting binding prices require the user's approval.

## Revenue operating rules

1. No fixed-cost commitments before revenue.
2. Prefer open data, open-source tools and existing free tiers.
3. Quote by defined deliverables and assumptions, not vague hours or unlimited revisions.
4. Collect a deposit or use a protected platform milestone before starting client-specific work.
5. Include data licensing, accuracy limitations, exclusions and acceptance criteria in every scope.
6. Track gross revenue, platform fees, taxes, delivery hours and net contribution.
7. Reinvest only from realised revenue, not anticipated sales.
