## About

Spatial Methods is an independent exploration and mastery environment for the spatial and computational world.

Start with a question, a technology, a paper, a dataset, a method or a real problem. We help you understand the territory, find and assess useful knowledge, choose a meaningful next action and follow connections as far as your curiosity takes you.

One subject can lead in many directions. You might want a clear explanation, a research map, a practical experiment, a teaching resource, working code or a way to apply a method. Spatial Methods supports these different intentions without forcing everyone through one course or curriculum.

### What you will find

- **Exploration Library:** question-led investigations connecting concepts, methods, tools, evidence and applications.
- **Curiosity Desk:** a place to suggest questions, resources, technologies and problems worth investigating.
- **Laboratory:** practical investigations, worked examples, notebooks, exercises and reproducible workflows where appropriate.
- **Research Dispatches:** evidence-led notes on research, technology, data, policy and emerging developments.
- **Connections:** routes into adjacent disciplines and deeper technical foundations.

We curate and contextualise excellent existing resources rather than pretending to replace universities, research groups, open-source communities or public data providers. When original work adds value, we create it and make its provenance clear.

The public GitHub repository remains the canonical home for versioned technical material, code, notebooks, references and corrections. Patreon is where members find guided routes, companion material and ways to participate.

## Frequently asked questions

**Do I need a geospatial or technical background?**  
No fixed background is assumed. Each exploration should make its prerequisites and technical depth visible. Some advanced routes will require mathematics, programming or domain knowledge, and we will say so.

**Is this a course platform?**  
Not in the conventional sense. We organise around questions and investigations, with optional routes for understanding, research, practice, teaching, building and application.

**Are the technical resources on GitHub?**  
Yes. GitHub is the canonical archive for versioned technical material. Patreon provides member navigation, editorial framing, companion work and participation.

**Can I suggest a topic?**  
Yes. Use the Curiosity Desk. Suggestions are assessed for relevance, evidence, scope, usefulness and feasibility; submission is not a promise of bespoke work or publication.

**Will every topic have practical exercises?**  
No. We add practical work when it genuinely helps answer the question and can be done responsibly. Some subjects are better served by a source map, explanation or research synthesis.

**How often is new material published?**  
We aim for a sustainable rhythm rather than a volume promise. Substantial explorations, research dispatches and practical work are published when they are ready and checked. Any scheduled sessions are announced separately.

**Can I use or teach from the material?**  
Check the licence and attribution information attached to each resource. Third-party material remains subject to its own terms. Do not assume every linked resource can be redistributed.
