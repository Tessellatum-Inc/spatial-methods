# Curiosity Desk — what should we investigate?

Spatial Methods grows through questions.

Send us something you have encountered that deserves a closer look: a concept, algorithm, paper, dataset, technology, map, tool, claim or real-world problem.

It can be broad, half-formed or difficult to name. You do not need to know the right terminology before asking.

## What makes a useful suggestion?

If you can, tell us:
- **What caught your attention?**
- **What are you trying to understand, investigate or accomplish?**
- **Is there a paper, dataset, example, place or link we should examine?**
- **What have you already tried or found confusing?**

A short question is enough to begin. Extra context helps us choose the right response.

## What happens next?

We review suggestions for:
- relevance to spatial and computational knowledge;
- whether credible evidence or data is available;
- whether the question can be scoped into a useful investigation;
- practical, research, teaching or application value;
- connections to existing material and the wider archive;
- the effort required to do the work properly.

A suggestion may lead to a concise answer, a curated resource shelf, a territory map, a practical investigation, a research dispatch, a new exploration—or a pointer to material that already answers it.

We may clarify, combine, defer or decline a suggestion. We will not promise a bespoke course, private consulting or guaranteed publication for every submission.

## Submit your question

Use the comments on this post or the available Patreon message route. Include links where useful, but do not share confidential, personal or restricted information.

If your question connects to an existing exploration, tell us where the connection appeared.

**The best starting point is simply: what have you been wondering about?**
