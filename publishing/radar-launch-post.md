# Radar: start with a question, follow the signal

Radar is a much bigger subject than most people first imagine.

A satellite uses radar to observe the Earth's surface. A weather service uses radar to understand precipitation. An engineer designs a system to detect and track targets. A researcher studies how electromagnetic signals interact with materials, or how phase measurements can reveal tiny changes in the ground.

These worlds share foundations, but they are not the same specialism.

We have opened the first Spatial Methods Radar exploration around a simple question:

**How can a sensor send out radio waves, receive the echoes and use them to tell us something about a surface—sometimes through cloud, at night, and without a camera?**

This is not a course you have to complete in order. It is a map you can use in the way that helps you.

## Choose your route

- **Get oriented:** understand the difference between optical remote sensing, radar and SAR.
- **Map the subject:** distinguish Earth-observation SAR, InSAR, polarimetry, weather radar, systems engineering and signal processing.
- **Try real data:** inspect a Sentinel-1 scene and record what the backscatter patterns might—and might not—mean.
- **Go technical:** follow image formation, signal processing, measurement geometry or the physics of scattering.
- **Apply it:** explore how radar supports flood observation, deformation monitoring, agriculture, forestry and other applications.

## Your first practical investigation

The companion Sentinel-1 investigation gives you two ways to begin:

1. Inspect a prepared radiometrically terrain-corrected product to focus on interpretation.
2. Work from a Sentinel-1 GRD product and learn more of the processing chain in SNAP.

You will record the scene, acquisition details, product type, polarisation, radiometric convention, processing history, observations and uncertainties. The point is not to label pixels by colour. It is to learn how to reason from a measurement.
