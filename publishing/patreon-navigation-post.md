# The Spatial Methods field guide

Use this post as a map of the member experience. These destinations are ways to find and use the work—not separate disciplines, learner types or mandatory stages.

## Start Here
Understand what Spatial Methods is, how the archive works and how to choose a useful first step. New members should begin with the pinned **Welcome to Spatial Methods** post.

## Exploration Library
Explore a growing collection of questions that connect ideas, methods, technologies, datasets, research and applications. Each investigation offers the routes that make sense for its subject; not every topic needs the same structure.

## Curiosity Desk
Suggest a question, paper, algorithm, dataset, tool or problem. We assess submissions and decide what form of response would be most useful. Open the pinned **Curiosity Desk — what should we investigate?** post.

## Laboratory
Find practical investigations, worked examples, notebooks and technical walkthroughs. The emphasis is on understanding what was done, why it was done, what the result shows and where it can fail.

## Research Dispatches
Follow selected developments and research questions through evidence-led notes. Dispatches should explain why a signal matters, what the sources establish and what remains uncertain.

## Where the technical work lives

The [Spatial Methods GitHub repository](https://github.com/Tessellatum-Inc/spatial-methods) is the canonical home for versioned technical content, code, notebooks, source references and corrections.

## How to use the field guide

Start with whatever has caught your attention. Move between the destinations as needed. A practical investigation may lead you back to a concept; a research dispatch may open a new exploration; a question from the Curiosity Desk may connect several existing topics.

There is no single correct route through the work.
